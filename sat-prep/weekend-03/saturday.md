# Weekend 3 — Saturday (4 hours)

**Theme:** PSDA — Problem-Solving and Data Analysis  
**Skills:** C1 ratios/rates/units · C2 percents · C3 one-variable data  
**Mode:** Wording first. These are not “new math.” They are SAT’s favorite way to hide easy arithmetic.

**What PSDA is:** College Board’s name for the ~15% of Math (5–7 questions) that is ratios, unit rates, percents, tables/graphs, probability, and statistical *claims*. A 680 scorer often “knows” this and still drops 2–3 raw points on wording.

All items are **instructor-created SAT-style**.

Whiteboard translation habits:

| English | Math move |
|---|---|
| per, each, rate | divide; units in the answer |
| of | multiply |
| increases by \(p\%\) | \(\times(1+p/100)\) |
| decreases by \(p\%\) | \(\times(1-p/100)\) |
| what percent of | part/whole \(\times 100\) |
| given the mean of \(n\) values | sum \(= n\cdot\text{mean}\) |

## Block 1 — Ratios, rates, units (0:15–1:20) · C1

### Oral
1. \(2:5\) with 12 on the first part — how do you set the proportion?  
2. 45 miles in 3/4 hour — mph?  
3. Two workers at different rates. Combined rate is **sum**, then time \(=\) work / combined.

### Items

**S1.** Mix is flour:sugar \(=2:5\). 12 cups flour. Cups of sugar?

**S2.** A car travels 180 miles in 3 hours. At that rate, miles in 2 hours 30 minutes? (SPR)

**S3.** Printer A: 40 pages/min. Printer B: 55 pages/min. Together, minutes to print 600 pages?

**S4.** A recipe for 8 servings uses 3 cups of broth. Cups of broth for 20 servings?

**S5.** Convert 90 minutes to hours, then find distance at 48 mph. (SPR, miles)

**S6.** Juice concentrate:water \(=1:3\). How many cups of concentrate in 16 cups of finished mix?

**S7.** (MC) Which rate is fastest?  
 A) 5 miles in 12 minutes B) 20 mph C) 8 miles in 20 minutes D) 0.4 miles/minute

### Nitpick
- Hours vs minutes (2.5 not 2:30 as 230)  
- Combined rate: add rates, **not** add times  
- Ratio parts: total parts first when the question gives the *whole*

## Block 2 — Percentages (1:20–2:15) · C2

### Oral
1. “40 is what percent of 160?” vs “40% of 160 is?”  
2. Up 25% then down 25% — back to start?  
3. Tax after discount: which price gets the tax?

### Items

**S8.** 40 is what percent of 160?

**S9.** 18 is 45% of what number? (SPR)

**S10.** \$80 increases 25%, then decreases 25%. Final price?

**S11.** A \$60 jacket is 15% off, then 8% tax on the **sale** price. Final price?

**S12.** A value increases from 50 to 65. Percent increase?

**S13.** A population is 800. It grows 10%, then grows 10% again. New population?  
 A) 960 B) 968 C) 880 D) 1000

**S14.** (SPR) What number, increased by 20%, becomes 72?

### Nitpick
- Percent change \(=\dfrac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\)  
- Successive 10% and 10% is \(\times 1.1\times 1.1=1.21\), not \(+20\%\)  
- “Increased by 20% becomes 72” → \(1.2x=72\), not \(x+20=72\)

## Block 3 — One-variable data (2:25–3:35) · C3

### Oral
1. Mean vs median vs range — one sentence each.  
2. Add a huge outlier. What happens to each?  
3. Mean of 5 scores is 80. What’s the sum? Why does SAT love that?

### Items

**S15.** Data: \(2,2,3,7,16\). Mean, median, range?

**S16.** Scores: \(70,74,74,76,92\). Mean vs median. Which is the better “typical” if you want to ignore the 92?

**S17.** Five tests, mean 80. The first four scores are 72, 88, 90, 70. What is the fifth? (SPR)

**S18.** List A: \(3,5,5,7,10\). List B: \(3,5,5,7,30\). Which statement is true?  
 A) Medians differ a lot B) Means differ a lot C) Ranges are equal D) Both means equal 6

**S19.** Adding 100 to the set \(2,2,3,7,16\) will  
 A) raise the median more than the mean  
 B) raise the mean more than the median  
 C) leave both unchanged  
 D) lower the range

**S20.** A histogram is piled on the left with a long tail to the right (right-skewed). Typically:  
 A) mean \(<\) median B) mean \(>\) median C) mean \(=\) median always D) range is 0

### Nitpick
- Odd count: median is the middle after **sorting**  
- Range \(=\) max − min, not “how many numbers”  
- Skew: tail pulls the **mean**
