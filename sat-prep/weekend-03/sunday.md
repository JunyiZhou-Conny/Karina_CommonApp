## Block 1 — Scatterplots and models (0:15–1:10) · C4

### Oral
1. Positive vs negative association.  
2. Line of best fit \(y=mx+b\): meaning of \(m\) and \(b\) in context.  
3. Linear vs exponential: constant difference vs constant ratio.  
4. A prediction *outside* the data range (extrapolation) — trust it?

### Items

**U1.** Best fit \(y=8x+42\) for study hours \(x\) and score \(y\). Interpret slope. Predict \(x=6\).

**U2.** Table: \(x=0,1,2,3\) and \(y=12,18,27,40.5\). Linear or exponential? Model?

**U3.** A scatterplot is tight around a rising line except one point far below. The line of best fit  
 A) is pulled a little toward that point B) ignores outliers always C) becomes vertical D) must pass through that point

**U4.** Which is a linear model for “starts at 80 L and drains 2.5 L/min”?  
 A) \(V=80-2.5t\) B) \(V=80(0.025)^t\) C) \(V=2.5-80t\) D) \(V=80/2.5t\)

**U5.** (SPR) Using \(C=12n+45\), cost of 8 shirts.

### Nitpick
- Slope units: “\(y\)-units per \(x\)-unit”  
- Don’t call an exponential table linear because it “goes up”

## Block 3 — Margin of error + study design (2:20–3:15) · C6 / C7

### Oral
1. Sample percent 52%, MOE 4%. Interval?  
2. Does MOE mean “the candidate wins”?  
3. Larger random sample → MOE usually **smaller**.  
4. Honors-hallway survey claiming “the school agrees” — what’s wrong?  
5. Observational study vs experiment (random assignment).

### Items

**U13.** 52% support, MOE 4%. Best conclusion?  
 A) Exactly 52% of all voters support it  
 B) True support is likely between 48% and 56%  
 C) The candidate will win  
 D) The sample is biased because it isn’t 50–50

**U14.** Two random samples, same population. Sample A \(n=100\), Sample B \(n=900\). Which likely has the **smaller** margin of error?  
 A) A B) B C) always equal D) cannot say without the percent

**U15.** A researcher asks only students in the honors hallway whether they like a new schedule, then claims the **whole school** supports it. The main problem is  
 A) the mean is undefined B) convenience / biased sample C) margin of error is 0 D) it is a well-designed experiment

**U16.** To test whether a new tutor program **causes** higher scores, you should  
 A) survey whoever already bought the program  
 B) randomly assign students to program vs not, then compare scores  
 C) interview only A+ students  
 D) use the median instead of the mean

**U17.** A news story says “people who eat more vegetables have lower blood pressure, so vegetables cause lower blood pressure.” The SAT-level issue:  
 A) percents cannot apply to health B) possible confounding (exercise, income, etc.) C) the median is larger D) MOE must be 50%

**U18.** A random sample of 400 students: 61% want later start, MOE 5%. Which is **not** justified?  
 A) The interval 56%–66% is a reasonable range for the true percent  
 B) More than half of all students might want a later start  
 C) Exactly 61% of all students want a later start  
 D) A larger sample would likely narrow the interval

### Nitpick
- C6 is almost always **interval language**, not certainty  
- C7 is **who was asked** and **was anything randomly assigned**
