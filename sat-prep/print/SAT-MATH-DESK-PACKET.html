1. Big picture

The SAT is now fully digital (U.S. since spring 2024). Students take it in College Board’s Bluebook app.

Section Modules Time Questions Score
Reading and Writing 2 × 32 min 64 min 54 (incl. pretest) 200–800
Math 2 × 35 min 70 min 44 (incl. pretest) 200–800
Break between sections 10 min — —

2. What changed vs the old paper SAT (important for instructors who took it ~5–6 years ago)

Old paper SAT (through ~2023) Digital SAT (now)
~3 hours ~2 hours 14 minutes
Separate No Calculator + Calculator Math Calculator allowed on all Math
Linear form (same difficulty for everyone) Multistage adaptive (Module 2 depends on Module 1)
Longer wordy stems Shorter, more focused questions
Grid-in answers on paper Student-produced response (SPR) in Bluebook
Bring your own calc Desmos graphing calculator built into Bluebook (+ optional personal approved calc)
Domains: Heart of Algebra, Passport to Advanced Math, etc. Renamed/refined: Algebra, Advanced Math, PSDA, Geometry & Trigonometry

Takeaway for teaching: do not drill “no-calculator tricks” as a separate half of Math. Teach Desmos fluency as a core skill.

3. How Math adaptive modules work

  1. Module 1: Mix of easy / medium / hard (routing module).
  2. Performance on Module 1 routes the student to a harder or easier Module 2.
  3. Within a module, questions generally go easier → harder.
  4. You can skip/flag and move freely inside a module; you cannot go back after the module timer ends.

Score implication: Missing easy Module 1 items can send her to the easier Module 2 and cap upside. Accuracy on the first ~10–12 “should-get” Algebra items matters a lot for a 780 push.

4. Math question formats

  • Multiple choice (~75%): 4 options (A–D).
  • Student-produced response / SPR (~25%): Type the answer (integer, decimal, or fraction per Bluebook rules). No choices — calculation care matters.

About 30% of Math questions are in context (science, social studies, or real-world scenarios). Shorter than old SAT word problems, but still require translating English → math.

5. Four Math content domains (College Board)

Domain Approx. % # questions What it tests
Algebra ~35% 13–15 Linear equations, inequalities, systems, linear functions
Advanced Math ~35% 13–15 Nonlinear: quadratic, exponential, absolute value, polynomials, rationals, radicals
Problem-Solving & Data Analysis (PSDA) ~15% 5–7 Ratios, %, units, data displays, probability, statistical claims

PSDA is not a separate school subject. It is College Board’s label for the “story + table + percent + probability + what can you conclude” questions. 6. Pacing target

  • Math: 70 minutes / 44 questions ≈ 1.6 minutes per question.
  • Strategy for ~680 → 780: 1. Bank easy Algebra early (high accuracy). 2. Use Desmos for graphs, systems, and “which equation matches” checks. 3. Don’t bleed 3+ minutes on one hard Advanced Math item before Module 1 is secure. 4. Always attempt SPRs — blank SPR is free miss.

7. Tools to master

  1. Bluebook — practice tests + UI (mark for review, answer eliminator, SPR entry).
  2. Desmos (in Bluebook) — graph lines/parabolas, solve systems visually, check intersections.
  3. Official Student Question Bank — filter by domain / skill / difficulty.
  4. Error log — every miss: skill tag + mistake type (concept / setup / arithmetic / rushed).

Block 1 — Algebra survey (0:20–1:10) · A1–A5

Oral Q&A (student first)

  1. You see (5-2(3x+1)=x-17). What is the first trap, before any arithmetic?
  2. When do two linear equations have no solution? When infinitely many?
  3. When must you flip an inequality? Give an example.
  4. In (C=12n+45), what do 12 and 45 mean? What question is SAT likely to ask besides “find (C)”?
  5. System in context: adult \$12, child \$7, 15 tickets, \$145. What are the two equations?

Rapid items

S1. (A1) (5-2(3x+1)=x-17)

S2. (A1, SPR) (\dfrac{x}{4}+\dfrac{x}{6}=5)

S3. (A4) How many solutions? (\begin{cases}2x-4y=6\ x-2y=5\end{cases})

S4. (A5) Solution set of (-2x+5>13):
 A) (x>-4) B) (x<-4) C) (x>4) D) (x<4)

S5. (A2–A3) Write (3x-2y=12) in slope-intercept form. State slope and (y)-intercept.

S6. (A3, SPR) Gym: \$40 signup + \$25/month. Total paid \$165. How many months?

Nitpick recap

  • Distributing a leading minus
  • LCD must hit every term
  • Parallel lines vs same line
  • Flip only when multiplying/dividing by a negative
  • Re-read: did they want (x), (y), or a count?

Block 2 — Advanced Math survey (1:10–2:00) · B1–B4

Oral Q&A (student first)

  1. SAT says “which expression is equivalent.” What is your fastest check besides expanding everything?
  2. How do you find the vertex of (y=ax^2+bx+c) without completing the square?
  3. Table: (y) goes 12, 18, 27, 40.5. Linear or exponential? How do you know in 15 seconds?
  4. Why can a rational equation grow an extraneous root?
  5. A line and a parabola: how many intersections are possible? How does Desmos settle it?

Rapid items

S7. (B1) Which is equivalent to (4x(x-3)-2(x-3))?
 A) (2(x-3)(2x-1)) B) (2(x-3)(2x+1)) C) ((x-3)(4x-2)) D) Both A and C

S8. (B4) Vertex of (y=-2(x+1)^2+5). Maximum or minimum?

S9. (B4) Which models exponential decay?
 A) (y=20-3x) B) (y=20(0.85)^x) C) (y=20(1.15)^x) D) (y=3x+20)

S10. (B2) Solve (|2x-3|=7).

S11. (B3) How many solutions: (y=x^2) and (y=x+2)?

S12. (B2, SPR) For (y=x^2-4x-1), the minimum value of (y) is?

Nitpick recap

  • Factor the common binomial before expanding
  • Vertex form: ((h,k)) is opposite the sign inside ((x-h))
  • Decay = multiply by a factor between 0 and 1, not “minus a constant”
  • Absolute value: two cases, then check
  • Nonlinear systems: algebra or Desmos — both, if time

Break (2:00–2:10)

Stretch + water. No phones if possible.

Block 3 — PSDA survey (2:10–3:00) · C1–C7

Say the name: Problem-Solving and Data Analysis. Not “statistics class.” SAT’s version is short and wording-heavy.

Oral Q&A (student first)

  1. “Increases by 20%” vs “increases by 20.” What’s the equation move?
  2. A value goes up 25% then down 25%. Back to start? Why or why not?
  3. When is median a better center than mean?
  4. Two-way table: what’s the difference between (P(\text{A})) and (P(\text{A}\mid\text{B}))?
  5. A poll is (52\%\pm 4\%). What can you conclude? What can you not?

Rapid items

S13. (C1) A mix uses flour:sugar (=2:5). If you use 12 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar?

S14. (C2) A price of \$80 increases 25%, then the new price decreases 25%. What is the final price?

S15. (C3) Data: (2,2,3,7,16). Mean and median? Which is pulled by the outlier?

S16. (C4) A scatterplot of study hours vs score is roughly linear and rising. The line of best fit is (y=8x+42). Interpret the slope. Predict the score at 6 hours.

S17. (C5) Club members:

Yes No Total
Junior 12 8 20
Senior 18 12 30
Total 30 20 50

What is the probability a randomly chosen senior said Yes?

S18. (C6–C7) A random sample of voters gives 52% support, margin of error 4%. Which conclusion is most appropriate?
