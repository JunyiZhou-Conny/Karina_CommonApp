**26.** Near the end of a city-council livestream, the host invited viewers to answer a poll on the show’s website: “Do you support the new parking rule discussed tonight?” The host later reported that \(31\%\) answered “Yes” and \(66\%\) answered “No.” Which of the following best explains why the results are unlikely to represent the residents of the city?

A) The percentages do not add up to \(100\%\), so any possible conclusions from the poll are invalid.  
B) Those who responded to the poll were not a random sample of the residents of the city.  
C) There were not \(50\%\) “Yes” responses and \(50\%\) “No” responses.  
D) The show did not allow viewers enough time to respond to the poll.

**27.** A botanist wants to know whether a new fertilizer changes tomato yield. She has \(40\) plants of the same variety. She assigns \(20\) plants at random to the new fertilizer and \(20\) to the old fertilizer, grows them in the same greenhouse, and compares the mean yield. Which statement is true?

A) This is an observational study, so a difference in mean yield cannot be used to claim the fertilizer caused the change.  
B) This is an experiment with random assignment, so a difference in mean yield can be used to claim the fertilizer caused the change.  
C) The results can be generalized to all plants of every species, because \(40\) is a large sample.  
D) The study is biased because the two groups do not have the same number of plants.

**28.** A clinic director selected \(80\) patients at random from **all** patients at the clinic. The table shows only the \(35\) patients in that sample who had at least \(4\) visits last year.

| Total | \(20\) | \(15\) | \(35\) |

Which of the following is the largest population to which the results of the study can be generalized?

A) All clinic patients who improved  
B) The \(80\) patients in the sample
