1. Notes for this set only

Successive percent

  • Each change multiplies. Do not add the percents.
  • Up p% means ×(1+100p​). Down p% means ×(1−100p​).
  • After you multiply, you already have the new amount. Do not add the starting amount back on. The grown number includes the start.
  • Two identical ups: square the factor. +20% then +20% is ×1.44, a 44% increase, not +40%.

Tangent to a circle — 0 vs undefined

  • The radius to the contact point P is perpendicular to the tangent at P.
  • A line perpendicular to the x-axis is vertical. Its slope is undefined.
  • A line parallel to the x-axis is horizontal. Its slope is 0.
  • Same x on center and P ⇒ radius is vertical ⇒ tangent is horizontal ⇒ slope 0.
  • Same y on center and P ⇒ radius is horizontal ⇒ tangent is vertical ⇒ slope undefined.
  • SAT asks for the tangent, not the radius. Circle that word before you bubble.

Right-triangle trig

  • Name the angle first. Opposite / adjacent / hypotenuse are relative to that angle.
  • sinθ=hypopp​, cosθ=hypadj​, tanθ=adjopp​. Isolate the side they asked for.
  • If the sides are ugly (165 and 220, 156 and 208), SAT wants the ratio. Those numbers cancel. Do not Pythagorean a giant hypotenuse first.

Messy estimate

  • The graph or intercept is allowed to be a little dirty. Compute, then take closest.
  • Write what the intercept means (all of one type, none of the other) before you divide.
  • The other intercept is the usual trap.

Linear systems — how many solutions

  • Same line (one equation is a scale of the other) ⇒ infinitely many.
  • Same slope, different intercept ⇒ none.
  • Different slopes ⇒ exactly one.
  • “At least one” means infinitely many or exactly one.

Area units

  • Length converts with k. Area converts with k2.
  • Square the linear factor before you multiply by the given area.

Two lines, four angles

  • Verticals are equal. Adjacent angles sum to 180∘.
  • There are only three possible pairwise sums. “Could NOT” means the choice that is not one of those three.
