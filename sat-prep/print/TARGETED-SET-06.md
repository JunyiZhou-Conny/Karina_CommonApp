## 1. Notes for this set only

### Percent less / percent greater

“\(p\%\) **less than** \(R\)” means the new amount is \(R\bigl(1-\frac{p}{100}\bigr)\). It is not \(R\cdot\frac{p}{100}\).

“\(p\%\) **greater than** \(C\)” means the new amount is \(C\bigl(1+\frac{p}{100}\bigr)\).

A sale price can be chained: first take the percent off the regular price, then set that sale equal to “percent greater than cost.” Do the two jobs in order.

### Histogram bins — smallest and largest mean

The bars only tell you **how many** values sit in each interval, not **which** integers. Inside a bin you may place every integer at the left end or the right end.

- **Smallest** \(|\text{mean }A-\text{mean }B|\): if the two histograms have the **same** frequencies, you can use the **same** 23 (or 25) integers in both sets. The difference of the means can be **0**.
- **Largest** difference: push set \(A\) as far right as the bins allow, and set \(B\) as far left (or the other way). Integers from \(20\) up to but not including \(30\) go from \(20\) to \(29\). The gap in every bin is \(9\), so the means differ by \(9\).

### Equilateral height, and isosceles right

An equilateral triangle with side \(s\) has height \(\dfrac{s\sqrt{3}}{2}\). If they write that height as \(k\sqrt{3}\), then \(k=\dfrac{s}{2}\). Perimeter \(3s\) gives \(s\) first.

An **isosceles right** triangle has two equal legs and a right angle between them. Legs \(s,s\), hypotenuse \(s\sqrt{2}\). The altitude to the hypotenuse is \(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}=\dfrac{s\sqrt{2}}{2}\).

### Factor \(x+2b\)

\((x+2b)\) is a factor of a quadratic only if \(x=-2b\) makes the quadratic \(0\). Plug \(x=-2b\) into each choice. You need a **positive integer** \(b\). A leftover that forces \(b=0\) or a non-integer is dead.

### Sphere volume (reference)

\[V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\]

The SAT prints this on the reference page. Cube the radius, then multiply. If they ask for \(V/\pi\), the \(\pi\) drops.

### Percent of last year vs percent of the start

- Each year the value increases by \(p\%\) **of its value the previous year** \(\Rightarrow\) multiply by \(1+\frac{p}{100}\) every year \(\Rightarrow\) **increasing exponential**.
- Each year it increases by \(p\%\) **of the initial amount** \(\Rightarrow\) add the **same** dollar amount every year \(\Rightarrow\) **increasing linear**.

Read which amount they percented. Decreasing versions flip the sign. Linear vs exponential is “add a constant” vs “multiply by a constant.”

### Scatterplot \(y=ax^2+bx+c\)

- **Sign of \(a\):** opens down \(\Rightarrow a<0\). Opens up \(\Rightarrow a>0\).
- **Sign of \(c\):** the \(y\)-intercept. Above the origin \(\Rightarrow c>0\). Below \(\Rightarrow c<0\).
- **Sign of \(b\):** the vertex is at \(x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\). If that \(x\) is **positive** and \(a<0\), then \(b\) must be **positive**. If that \(x\) is **negative** and \(a<0\), then \(b\) is **negative**.

Write the three signs before you hunt the matching equation.

### Quadratic sold-vs-price — minimum is still a vertex

“Minimum of \(k\) sold at price \(h\)” is the vertex \((h,k)\), and \(a>0\). One more point finds \(a\).

\[N(p)=a(p-h)^2+k\]

The graph is symmetric about \(p=h\). Prices the same distance from \(h\) sell the **same** number. A line through two prices is the wrong model.

### Volunteer poll vs random sample

A website / call-in / “viewers go to our site” poll is **not** a random sample. Percents that add to 98 instead of 100 do **not** kill the poll.

If they selected people **at random from a whole population**, you may generalize to that **whole population** (the sampling frame). A table that later shows only a subset does not shrink the frame.

Random assignment of a treatment is an **experiment** and can support a cause. A survey that just watches who already does what is **observational** and cannot.

### Point-slope constant is \(F(h)\), not \(F(0)\)

\[F(x)=A+m(x-h)\]

The printed \(A\) is the value when \(x=h\). Convert \(h\) back to a date or a clock time. \(F(0)\) is a different plug-in: \(A+m(0-h)=A-mh\).

### Exponential: \(p\%\) less than the last value

\[f(x)=A(r)^x,\qquad 0<r<1\]

Then \(f(n)=r\cdot f(n-1)\), so \(f(n)\) is \((1-r)\cdot 100\%\) **less** than \(f(n-1)\). The leftover \(r\) (for example \(0.28\)) is **not** \(p\). \(p=72\), not \(28\).
