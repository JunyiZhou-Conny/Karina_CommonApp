## Block 1 — Area and volume (0:15–1:15) · D1

### Items

**S1.** Cube edge 3. Volume? If edge doubles, new volume?

**S2.** Rectangle 8 by 6. Area? Diagonal? (SPR for the diagonal)

**S3.** Triangle base 10, height 7. Area?

**S4.** A rectangular prism is 4 by 3 by 5. Volume? Surface area?

**S5.** Two similar paintings. Side lengths in ratio \(2:3\). If the smaller area is 20, the larger area is?  
 A) 30 B) 40 C) 45 D) 60

**S6.** A cylinder has radius 3 and height 4. Volume in terms of \(\pi\)? (SPR: enter the coefficient of \(\pi\))

**S7.** Every length of a sculpture is scaled by \(\tfrac12\). The new volume is what fraction of the original?  
 A) \(\tfrac12\) B) \(\tfrac14\) C) \(\tfrac18\) D) \(\tfrac{1}{16}\)

### Nitpick
- Area uses **two** dimensions (\(k^2\)), volume three (\(k^3\))  
- Cylinder: \(V=\pi r^2 h\), not \(\pi r h\)  
- Surface area: 2 of each pair of faces

## Block 2 — Angles, parallel lines, similar triangles (1:15–2:15) · D2

### Oral
1. Parallel lines + transversal: which angles are equal?  
2. Similar triangles: equal **angles**, proportional **sides**.  
3. Corresponding sides sit across from corresponding angles.

### Items

**S8.** Triangle angles \(40^\circ\) and \(65^\circ\). Third angle?

**S9.** Two similar triangles. Corresponding sides 6 and 8. Side 9 on the smaller corresponds to what on the larger?

**S10.** Similar triangles, corresponding sides 4 and 10. Smaller perimeter 18. Larger perimeter?

**S11.** Parallel lines cut by a transversal. One interior angle is \(110^\circ\). The alternate interior angle is?  
 A) \(70^\circ\) B) \(110^\circ\) C) \(20^\circ\) D) \(180^\circ\)

**S12.** A triangle has angles \(x\), \(2x\), and \(3x\). Find \(x\). (SPR)

**S13.** An isosceles triangle has vertex angle \(40^\circ\). A base angle is?

**S14.** (MC) Similar figures, scale factor \(3\). A 5° angle on the small figure becomes  
 A) \(15^\circ\) B) \(5^\circ\) C) \(45^\circ\) D) \(3^\circ\)

### Nitpick
- **Angles do not scale.** Similarity copies angles.  
- Perimeter scales by \(k\); area by \(k^2\).

## Block 3 — Pythagorean and special rights (2:25–3:35) · D3

### Oral
1. When can you skip \(a^2+b^2=c^2\) and just use a pattern?  
2. 45-45-90: legs \(s\), hyp \(s\sqrt{2}\).  
3. 30-60-90: short \(s\), long \(s\sqrt{3}\), hyp \(2s\).  
4. Short leg is opposite 30°.

### Items

**S15.** Legs 5 and 12. Hypotenuse?

**S16.** Isosceles right triangle, leg 5. Hypotenuse?

**S17.** 30-60-90, short leg 6. Long leg and hypotenuse?

**S18.** Rectangle 5 by 12. Diagonal?

**S19.** A right triangle has hyp 10 and one leg 6. The other leg? (SPR)

**S20.** 45-45-90, hypotenuse \(6\sqrt{2}\). A leg is?  
 A) 6 B) 3 C) \(6\sqrt{2}\) D) 12

**S21.** 30-60-90, hypotenuse 14. Short leg?  
 A) 7 B) \(7\sqrt{3}\) C) 14 D) \(14\sqrt{3}\)

**S22.** (SPR) Right triangle legs 9 and 12. Hypotenuse. (Recognize the 3-4-5 multiple.)

### Nitpick
- 6-8-10 and 9-12-15 are 3-4-5  
- 5-12-13 and 9-12-15 are different families — don’t mash them  
- Hypotenuse is **longest**; don’t put \(2s\) on a leg
