## Block 1 — Equivalent expressions, harder (0:15–1:15) · B1

### Oral (student first)

1. Fastest SAT check for “which is equivalent”: expand all, factor the stem, or Desmos overlay? When each?  
2. Why must you state \(x\neq 3\) when canceling \((x-3)\)? Does SAT still call the simplified form equivalent?  
3. Difference of cubes: \(a^3-b^3=\) ?

### Reminder (2 min, then items)
Factor / expand / cancel common factors / exponent rules. Desmos: identical graphs ⇒ equivalent (watch domain holes).

### Items

**S1.** Which is equivalent to \(\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}\) for \(x\neq 3\)?  
 A) \(x-3\) B) \(x+3\) C) \(x^2-3\) D) \(3-x\)

**S2.** Simplify \(\dfrac{2x^3-8x}{2x}\) for \(x\neq 0\).

**S3.** Factor completely: \(8x^3-27\).

**S4.** Which is equivalent to \(4x(x-3)-2(x-3)\)?  
 A) \(2(x-3)(2x-1)\) B) \(2(x-3)(2x+1)\) C) \(4x^2-10x+6\) D) \(2(x-3)(2x-1)+1\)

**S5.** (SPR) If \((3x^2 y)^2 = ax^b y^c\) and \(a,b,c>0\), what is \(a+b+c\)?

**S6.** Rewrite \(\sqrt{18x^2}\) as a simplified radical. Assume \(x>0\).

### Nitpick
- Cancel **factors**, not terms (\(x^2-9\) is not \(x^2\) over 9)  
- Cubes: \(8x^3-27=(2x-3)(4x^2+6x+9)\) — the quadratic does **not** factor over the reals  
- \((3x^2 y)^2=9x^4 y^2\), not \(3x^4 y^2\)

## Block 2 — Absolute value (1:15–2:05) · B2

### Oral
1. \(|A|=k\) with \(k>0\) means how many cases? If \(k=0\)? If \(k<0\)?  
2. \(|A|<3\) as a compound inequality?  
3. \(|A|=|B|\) — what’s the clean case split?

### Items

**S7.** Solve \(|3x+1|=10\).

**S8.** Solve \(|x-4|<3\). Write as a compound inequality.

**S9.** Solve \(|2x-5|=|x+1|\).

**S10.** How many solutions: \(|x-2|=-5\)?  
 A) 0 B) 1 C) 2 D) infinitely many

**S11.** (SPR) The larger solution of \(|2x-3|=7\).

### Nitpick
- Absolute value is never negative — S10 is a freebie if she remembers that  
- After solving cases, **check** (especially with variables on both sides)

## Block 3 — Rational equations (2:15–3:10) · B2

### Oral
1. First move on a rational equation?  
2. What value is never allowed?  
3. You solve and get the forbidden value. What do you write?

### Reminder
Multiply through by the LCD. Discard any solution that makes a denominator 0.

### Items

**S12.** Solve \(\dfrac{x+3}{x-1}=2\).

**S13.** Solve \(\dfrac{1}{x-2}+3=\dfrac{5}{x-2}\).

**S14.** Solve \(\dfrac{x}{x-1}=\dfrac{2}{x-1}+1\). How many solutions?

**S15.** For which \(x\) is \(\dfrac{x^2-5x+6}{x-2}\) undefined? What is the simplified expression for all other \(x\)?

**S16.** (SPR) Solution of \(\dfrac{4}{x}+\dfrac{1}{2}=3\). (Assume one real solution.)

### Nitpick
- S14 is a **no-solution** trap: algebra can look like it “cancels” into a contradiction  
- Undefined at the hole vs removable discontinuity — SAT usually asks “undefined” or “equivalent for \(x\neq\)”

## Block 4 — Radical equations (3:10–3:50) · B2

### Oral
1. Why square both sides? What extra root can appear?  
2. After solving, what **must** you do?  
3. \(\sqrt{\text{something}}\) is never negative. How does that kill a candidate?

### Items

**S17.** Solve \(\sqrt{2x+3}=x\). Check every candidate.

**S18.** Solve \(\sqrt{x+12}=x\).

**S19.** Solve \(\sqrt{3x-2}=4\).

**S20.** (SPR) The **larger** valid solution of \(\sqrt{5x+1}=x+1\). If none, enter 0.

### Nitpick
- Isolate the radical **before** squaring  
- Check is not optional — this is the SAT skill
